# ACT FIVE — "THE VALLEY"

---

## SCENE 12 — "AM I A DOG"

---

**EXT. VALLEY OF ELAH, THE FLOOR — DAY**

Both armies have come to the edges of their ridges. They line the slopes on
either side — thousands of men on the left in the dun and iron of Israel,
thousands on the right in the bronze of Philistia — two walls of held breath
facing each other across the open ground. Above them, a white sky. No wind now.
The dust hangs where it was kicked and does not settle. Heat presses down on
everything like a hand.

And into the middle of it all, alone, walks a boy with a stick.

The Israelite line watches him go with a kind of sick disbelief. This is not a
champion. This is a shepherd. Some of them cover their eyes. Eliab, on the line,
cannot watch and cannot look away.

On the far side, the giant's SHIELD-BEARER walks a few paces ahead of his
master, and it is the shield-bearer who first understands, who stops, who says
something back over his shoulder. And GOLIATH — who had been waiting for a man
in armor, for a captain, for someone worth the morning — looks down into the
valley and sees what is actually coming.

A boy. Unarmored. A staff. A bag.

And Goliath laughs.

It starts low, in the chest, and it builds, and it rolls out across the whole
valley — a huge, delighted, genuinely amused laugh, the laugh of a man who has
been handed an insult so complete it has become a gift. The Philistine line
catches it and laughs with him, ten thousand men, and the sound rolls up the
Israelite slope and lands on every ashamed face over there.

David keeps walking. Says nothing. Lets him laugh.

Goliath comes down to meet him, the shield-bearer scrambling to keep the great
shield between his master and the valley. He stops perhaps thirty paces off. Up
close, the scale is obscene — the boy's head does not reach the giant's chest.
Goliath looks down the length of himself at David, and the laugh fades into
something worse: a smile.

> **GOLIATH** *(almost gentle, savoring it)*
> Am I a dog?

*A beat. He tilts his head.*

> **GOLIATH (CONT'D)**
> That you come at me with sticks?

David stops. Plants his staff. Says nothing yet. Just looks up at him — steady,
unhurried, present — the same face he wore at the brook.

Goliath waits for fear. It does not come. And something flickers behind the
giant's eyes — the smallest thing, a fly-speck of doubt — because he has done
this forty mornings and every man who came near him *smelled* of terror, and
this one does not, and he does not like it.

He covers it with contempt. Lifts his arms to the bronze gods on the ridges
behind him. Raises his voice for the whole valley to hear:

> **GOLIATH (CONT'D)**
> Come here, then, boy. *(a cruel smile)* Come to me, and I will give your flesh
> to the birds of the air. And the beasts of the field.

The Philistine line roars.

Silence from David. He lets the roar die. He lets it die all the way down,
until the valley is quiet again and the giant is left standing in his own echo
— and only then, into that silence, David speaks. Not loud. He does not need to
be loud. His voice is level and it carries, and it is the calm that unnerves,
not the volume.

> **DAVID**
> You come to me with a sword. *(beat)* And a spear. *(beat)* And a javelin.

He takes one step closer. Just one.

> **DAVID (CONT'D)**
> I come to you in the name of the LORD of hosts. The God of the armies of
> Israel. *(a beat)* The one you have been mocking. Every morning. For forty
> days.

The flicker behind Goliath's eyes again — larger this time. The smile thins.

> **DAVID (CONT'D)** *(still calm, still quiet, and this is the blade)*
> This day the LORD will hand you over to me. And I will strike you down. And I
> will take your head off your shoulders. *(beat)* And the birds you spoke of —
> *(the smallest tilt of his head, giving the giant his own words back)* — the
> birds will eat *you.*

For the first time in forty days, Goliath is not the one in control of the
conversation. His jaw sets. The mockery is gone; what's underneath it is
uglier and more human — insult, and the first cold thread of a feeling he has
not felt in years and cannot name in front of two armies.

David does not stop. His voice stays even, and somehow that is the most
frightening thing in the valley.

> **DAVID (CONT'D)**
> So that everyone here — *(he does not look away from the giant, but the words
> are for both armies)* — all of them, both sides, will know there is a God in
> Israel. *(quietly)* The battle is His. And He has already given you to us.

A silence you could break stone against.

Goliath's face works. Rage wins — because rage is all he has left to reach for.
He snaps his visor down. Snarls something wordless. Hefts the great spear and
starts forward, the shield-bearer scrambling aside, the giant's whole bronze
weight beginning to move down the slope toward the boy —

— and the boy does not back away.

The boy runs *at* him.

**HARD CUT — SILENCE ON THE ARMIES.**

## SCENE 13 — "THE STONE"

---

**EXT. VALLEY FLOOR — CONTINUOUS**

*No music. No slow motion. Real time. Every motion clean and fast and exact.*

David runs.

Not away — *toward.* Straight at the giant, closing the ground, and the sight of
it is so wrong that Goliath's charge hitches for half a step — nobody runs
*toward* him — and that half-step is everything.

David's right hand is already moving. As he runs he pulls a stone from the bag
at his hip — one motion, no fumble, the craftsman's hand that chose it at the
brook — and drops it into the pouch of the sling in his other hand.

Three more strides. He does not slow to throw. He throws *while running,* the way
he has ten thousand times on empty hillsides with no one watching.

His arm comes up and over. Once around — the sling whips a full circle, the leather
singing, the pouch a blur at the end of it — and on the down-stroke, at the exact
bottom of the arc, at full extension, at a dead run, he releases one cord.

The stone leaves the sling.

**A single frame of nothing.**

The stone crosses the air between them — fast, flat, faster than the eye wants to
follow — and there is no sound in the whole valley, twenty thousand men and not
one breath, just a small dark thing traveling very fast across a piece of empty
sky —

**IMPACT.**

It strikes Goliath in the forehead, below the rim of the bronze helmet, in the
one hand's-breadth of skin the armor does not cover.

The sound it makes is small. That is the horror of it. Not a crash — a *crack,*
sharp and final and almost quiet, the sound of a stone meeting bone.

Goliath stops.

The giant simply — stops. The spear-arm, mid-stride, goes slack. The great head
tips, just slightly, as if he has heard someone call his name from very far away.
For one endless second he stands there, enormous, upright, already gone but not
yet fallen, a tower with its foundation struck out from under it.

Then he goes down.

Not gracefully. Not slowly. All at once, all of it, forward — a mountain of bronze
and muscle collapsing onto its own face in the dust, and the ground *takes* the
impact, a deep concussive *whump* that the front ranks of both armies feel through
their feet.

And then — nothing.

**WIDE — THE VALLEY.**

Silence. Absolute. The dust rolls up slowly from where the giant fell and drifts
sideways in the still air. Nobody moves. Not one man on either ridge. Twenty
thousand soldiers stand frozen, staring down at the impossible thing in the
middle of the valley — the small figure, standing; the huge figure, down — and no
one can make it be real. No one cheers. No one can. The Philistines cannot
comprehend it and the Israelites do not dare believe it. The whole world holds
absolutely still.

David stands where he threw. Chest heaving. The empty sling hanging from his hand.
He is looking at the fallen giant with an expression that is not triumph — it is
almost the old confusion again, the shepherd's quiet astonishment at a thing that
should have killed him and did not. *Something held it off me. Every time I
called.*

Then he moves.

He walks — not runs, *walks,* deliberate, into the terrible stillness — across the
open ground toward the body. Every eye in the valley tracks him. He reaches the
giant, this fallen wall of a man, and he stands over him a moment, small beside
the ruin of him.

He bends. Takes hold of the giant's own SWORD — a blade so large David needs both
hands to draw it — and pulls it ringing from its sheath.

He lifts it.

*We do not need to see the stroke. The camera stays on the watching armies — on
the faces — as the blade comes down out of frame, and a sound, and the last
silence breaks.*

David straightens. And he lifts, high, in one blood-streaked hand, the thing that
ends the war.

**AND THE VALLEY ERUPTS.**

It comes all at once — the entire Israelite ridge breaks open into a single vast
roar that seems to come up out of the ground itself, forty days of shame and
terror detonating into sound. Men who a moment ago could not look at the giant are
suddenly pouring down the slope, weapons up, screaming. And across the valley the
Philistine line — which has watched its god die on the dust — breaks the other
way. Turns. Runs. An entire army, routing, before the boy has even lowered his
arm.

**CLOSE — DAVID.**

In the middle of the breaking world — the roar, the charge, the rout, thousands of
men flooding past him on both sides — David stands very still. Sword down now. And
he is not looking at the fleeing enemy, or the charging army, or the body at his
feet.

He has tipped his face up. Eyes closed. To the white sky. To the wind that has
started to move again through the terebinth trees. To the only one he was ever
really talking to.

He says nothing. His lips do not move. But we have been with him long enough, at
the fold and the fire and the brook, to know exactly what the silence is.

*Thank You.*

**PULL BACK — HIGH AND WIDE**

Up and up, until David is small again, a single still point in a valley that has
come violently alive around him — two armies in full flight and pursuit, dust
rising in a golden wall, the whole earth in motion —

— and one boy, standing at the center of it, face turned to the sky.

**HOLD. Then —**

**CUT TO BLACK.**

*END OF SCENE 13.*

and quietly asks the man beside him a question that will change both their lives:*

> **SAUL** *(O.S.)*
> ...Whose son is that?
