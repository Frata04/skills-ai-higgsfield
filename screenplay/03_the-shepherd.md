# ACT TWO — "THE SHEPHERD"

*Several years have passed. The oil is a long time dry. No one has come back
for the boy. He is nineteen, maybe twenty now — and he is still on the hills.*

---

## SCENE 3 — "THE COUNTING"

---

**EXT. THE GREZEB PASTURE, HILLS OF JUDAH — MIDDAY**

High summer. A wide bowl of yellow grass between two ridges, heat shimmering off
the stone. The flock — a hundred head, maybe more — grazes in loose knots. The
only sounds are bells, the tearing of grass, insects.

DAVID — twenty now. Broader than the boy we met, sun-blackened, hands rope-
scarred, but the same open face. He sits on a boulder, one knee up, whittling a
new pin for his staff with an easy, practiced boredom.

Below him, running himself ragged, is NADAV — twelve. All elbows and effort.
David's father hired the boy's family this spring; David is teaching him. Nadav
is trying to count the flock and losing. He keeps starting over, lips moving,
finger stabbing the air.

> **NADAV** *(frustrated, near tears)*
> — forty, forty-one — no — *(he stops)* — one of them keeps *moving.*

> **DAVID** *(not looking up from his whittling)*
> They do that. It's the one thing they're good at.

> **NADAV**
> How do *you* do it? You never even count. You just — look at them and you know.

David lowers the knife. Considers the boy. Then he hops off the rock and comes
down, unhurried.

> **DAVID**
> Because I'm not counting sheep. *(he gestures at the flock)* Nobody can count
> sheep. They won't hold still to be numbers.

He crouches beside the boy, points.

> **DAVID (CONT'D)**
> That one — the ewe with the split ear. She fought a bramble two years ago and
> lost. She always grazes uphill of the rest; she thinks she's in charge. If
> she's there, half the flock's accounted for, because they follow her.

He points again, to a small knot at the edge.

> **DAVID (CONT'D)**
> Those three are her daughters. Nervous, all of them. And that one —
> *(softer)* — the little dark one hanging back — that's the runt. She was born
> wrong in the leg. She'll always be last. So I don't look for a hundred sheep.
> I look for *her.* If the last one's safe, they're all safe.

Nadav stares at the dark ewe, then at David, with the open worship of a boy for
an older boy who knows things.

> **NADAV**
> What's her name?

> **DAVID** *(a small grin)*
> They don't have names. There's a hundred of them.

> **NADAV**
> But you just —

> **DAVID**
> *(caught, laughing)* — Fine. That one's Dust. Because she rolls in it when
> she's happy and she comes back the color of the ground.

Nadav laughs — a real, delighted boy's laugh — and David laughs with him, and
for a moment the two of them are just two kids on a hillside, the younger one
adoring the older, the older one gentler than the sun-hard hands would suggest.

Then David's face changes. Small. He's looking past Nadav, out toward the north
ridge where the grass gives way to a black seam of caves and thorn.

> **NADAV** *(following his gaze)*
> What?

> **DAVID** *(easy, but his eyes don't move)*
> Nothing. *(beat)* Keep them off the north line today. Down toward the water.

> **NADAV**
> Why?

David finally looks back at him. Smiles. Doesn't answer the real question.

> **DAVID**
> Because I said so, and I'm bigger than you.

He ruffles the boy's hair, stands, and the whole time his eyes drift back — once
— to the black line of the caves. Something up there. Not today. But soon.

## SCENE 4 — "THE NIGHT WATCH"

---

**EXT. SHEEPFOLD ON THE RIDGE — NIGHT**

A low stone fold, the flock a pale mass sleeping inside it. Beyond, the whole
sky — the huge, unpolluted, star-crowded dark of the ancient world, so bright it
throws shadows. A small fire. The bells silent. Nadav asleep on a folded cloak,
dead to the world, one arm flung out.

David sits awake against the stone wall, a wooden LYRE across his knee — simple,
handmade, one string bound with a twist of gut where it broke and was mended.
He is not performing. There is no one to perform for. He plays the way other men
breathe.

He plucks a low line — searching, wandering — and under it, half-voiced, barely
above the fire's crackle, he begins to sing. Not grand. A man talking to someone
he trusts in the dark.

> **DAVID** *(singing, low)*
> *The LORD is my shepherd...*
> *I shall not want.*

He stops. Listens to it. Tries it again, a hair different, working the words like
the pin he whittled — finding the true shape of the thing.

> **DAVID (CONT'D)** *(singing)*
> *He makes me lie down in green pastures...*
> *He leads me beside still waters.*

He glances, without thinking, at the sleeping flock. At the dark runt breathing
at the edge of them. A private smile — *I know exactly what that's like, being
led when you don't understand where.*

The song trails off. He sets his hand flat on the strings to still them. And now
he simply *talks* — quiet, unguarded, the way you only speak when you are
certain you are alone. Not reciting. Complaining, a little. Confiding.

> **DAVID** *(to the sky, plainly)*
> They anointed me. *(a breath, almost a laugh)* You remember. The old man. The
> oil. I was — what, sixteen? I thought — *(he shakes his head)* — I don't know
> what I thought. That someone would come.

He picks at a loose thread on his sleeve.

> **DAVID (CONT'D)**
> Four winters. Nobody's come. My brothers won't even say it out loud, they just
> — look at me. Like I made it up. Like I put the oil on my own head.

A long silence. The fire pops. He is not angry. He's something quieter and
harder — a young man carrying a promise nobody else believes, out on a hill in
the dark, alone.

> **DAVID (CONT'D)** *(softer, no bitterness in it now)*
> But You were there. I felt it. *(a pause)* So I'll wait. I'm good at waiting.
> *(the smallest grin)* Ask the sheep.

He lifts his hand off the strings. Plays one more phrase — resolved, at peace,
lovely — and lets it ring out into the enormous night.

For a moment: total stillness. Firelight. Stars. A boy, a lyre, a promise, and
God.

And then — the flock moves.

Not much. A ripple. A single ewe lifts her head, ears swivelling. Then another.
The pale mass tightens, the way water tightens before it's disturbed. One bell
clanks, sharp, wrong, in the silence.

David's hand freezes on the strings. His head comes up. The peace is gone from
his face — replaced, instantly, by something older, harder, wide awake.

He is very still. Listening.

Out beyond the firelight, in the black grass to the north — where the caves are —
something has begun to move against the wind.

## SCENE 5 — "THE THING IN THE DARK"

---

**EXT. SHEEPFOLD — CONTINUOUS — NIGHT**

David is on his feet without seeming to have stood. The lyre is on the ground.
His hand has found his staff — and, unhurried, the sling at his belt. He does
not shout. He does not wake the boy. He moves *toward* the sound, not away from
it, three slow steps to the low wall, putting his own body between the dark and
the flock.

The sheep are fully awake now, packing to the far side of the fold, climbing
each other, the panic starting to spread. Bells clattering. Dust rising pale in
the starlight.

David scans the black. Nothing. Nothing. The wind pushes the grass in long
silver waves and every wave looks like it could be —

*There.*

A shape. Low. Enormous. Moving along the outside of the fold with a terrible,
patient, liquid ease. Not hurrying. It has done this before. Starlight catches
two eyes — flat, gold, utterly without fear — and the long muscle of a shoulder.

A LION.

It stops. It has seen him. A young man with a stick, between it and a hundred
soft, penned, panicking animals. It is not impressed. Its head lowers. A sound
comes out of it that is felt in the chest before it's heard — a low, grinding,
proprietary growl. *This is mine now.*

Behind David, small and terrified, a voice — Nadav, awake, frozen, half-risen
from his cloak:

> **NADAV** *(a strangled whisper)*
> David — *David —* run —

David does not turn around. He does not run. His eyes stay locked on the gold
eyes in the dark. When he speaks it is low, and level, and it is not to Nadav.
It's to the lion.

> **DAVID** *(quiet, steady, almost gentle)*
> No.

The lion's muscles gather.

> **DAVID (CONT'D)** *(barely breathing it)*
> Not the last one.

And it springs.

**HARD CUT TO BLACK.**

*A beat of pure darkness and sound — the roar, the scream of sheep, a boy's
shout, a single struck note as something crashes into the lyre —*

*and then silence.*
